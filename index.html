Free patent bar practice questions with MPEP citations

The USPTO registration examination is open book, and the book is the MPEP. The USPTO states that the current examination covers the MPEP, Ninth Edition, Revision 01.2024. What it really tests is whether you can find the governing passage fast and read it precisely.

Below are ten questions in the exam format, five choices, one correct. Each answer names the MPEP section or 37 CFR rule it rests on and quotes the passage word for word. Every quotation was checked by script against the official MPEP text and checked to sit under the section it names, so a search in your own MPEP lands on it.

Question 1, Micro entity application limit

Inventor Iris Kowalski qualifies as a small entity and meets the gross income limit. She has previously been named as an inventor on seven applications, namely three U.S. provisional applications, two applications filed only in Canada, and two U.S. nonprovisional applications. She has not assigned or licensed any rights. Which of the following is correct regarding micro entity status on the gross income basis for her new application?

  1. (A) She cannot certify micro entity status because she has been named on more than four previously filed applications.
  2. (B) She can certify only if she also qualifies on the education basis.
  3. (C) She cannot certify, because the Canadian applications count toward the limit.
  4. (D) She cannot certify, because provisional applications count toward the limit.
  5. (E) She can certify micro entity status, because provisional applications and applications filed in another country do not count toward the four application limit.
Answer and explanation

Answer (E). 37 CFR 1.29(a)(2) excludes applications filed in another country, provisional applications, and international applications for which the basic national fee was not paid from the four application limit. Only Iris's two U.S. nonprovisional applications count, so she is within the limit and E is correct. A, C and D ignore these exclusions. B is wrong because the gross income basis and the education basis are independent alternatives.

MPEP 509.04(a), "Neither the applicant nor the inventor nor a joint inventor has been named as the inventor or a joint inventor on more than four previously filed patent applications, other than applications filed in another country, provisional applications under 35 U.S.C. 111(b), or international applications for which the basic national fee under 35 U.S.C. 41(a) was not paid;"

Question 2, Who may request ex parte reexamination

Which of the following is NOT in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP regarding requests for ex parte reexamination under 35 U.S.C. 302?

  1. (A) A corporation or a governmental entity may file a request
  2. (B) The patent owner may file a request
  3. (C) On request, the Office will keep the name of the person who files the request in confidence
  4. (D) An attorney may file a request without identifying the real client in interest
  5. (E) A request may be filed at any time during the period of enforceability of the patent
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). MPEP 2212 states that the name of the person who files the request will not be maintained in confidence, so C is the incorrect statement. Any person may file, including corporations, governmental entities and the patent owner, so A and B are correct. The persons likely to use reexamination include attorneys without identification of their real client in interest, so D is correct. 37 CFR 1.510(a) allows a request at any time during the period of enforceability, so E is correct.

MPEP 2212, "Some of the persons likely to use reexamination under 35 U.S.C. 302 are patentees, licensees, potential licensees, attorneys without identification of their real client in interest, infringers, potential exporters, patent litigants, interference applicants, and International Trade Commission respondents. The name of the person who files the request will not be maintained in confidence."

Question 3, Design patent term and related rules

Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP regarding a U.S. design patent issuing from an application filed under 35 U.S.C. 111 on June 1, 2020?

  1. (A) The patent has a term of fourteen years from the date of grant.
  2. (B) The patent has a term of fifteen years from the date of grant.
  3. (C) The patent has a term ending twenty years from the filing date.
  4. (D) The patent remains in force only if maintenance fees are paid 3 1/2, 7 1/2 and 11 1/2 years after grant.
  5. (E) The application could have claimed the benefit of an earlier provisional application under 35 U.S.C. 119(e).
Answer and explanation

Answer (B). Design patents issuing from applications filed on or after May 13, 2015 have a fifteen year term from grant under 35 U.S.C. 173. A applies only to applications filed before May 13, 2015. C describes the utility term of 35 U.S.C. 154. D is wrong, no maintenance fees are required for design patents. E is wrong, 35 U.S.C. 172 makes 119(e) inapplicable to designs.

MPEP 1505, "U.S. design patents resulting from applications filed on or after May 13, 2015 have a 15 year term from the date of grant."

Question 4, Preemption is not a standalone test

In response to a rejection under 35 U.S.C. 101, the applicant argues that the claim recites a mathematical formula but is eligible because it does not preempt every use of that formula, and attaches a declaration listing other ways to use the formula. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The argument is dispositive, because preemption is the sole test for eligibility.
  2. (B) The argument fails because a judicial exception must be old and long prevalent to be ineligible.
  3. (C) Preemption is the concern underlying the judicial exceptions, but it is not a standalone test, and the absence of complete preemption does not show that the claim is eligible.
  4. (D) The examiner must withdraw the rejection, because a narrow formula with limited applications can never be a judicial exception.
  5. (E) The argument should be evaluated only under Step 1.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). The MPEP states that preemption is not a standalone test and that questions of preemption are resolved within the Alice/Mayo framework, so lack of complete preemption does not establish eligibility. A is wrong for the same reason. B is wrong because newly discovered exceptions are still exceptions. D is wrong because even narrow laws or formulas may not be preempted, as in Mayo and Flook. E is wrong because preemption concerns are addressed in Steps 2A and 2B, not Step 1.

MPEP 2106.04, "While preemption is the concern underlying the judicial exceptions, it is not a standalone test for determining eligibility."

Question 5, Modification rendering prior art unsatisfactory for its intended purpose

Applicant claims a blood filter with the inlet and outlet at the bottom and a gas vent at the top. The examiner relies on a gasoline strainer whose inlet and outlet are at the top and which uses gravity to collect dirt and water at the bottom, reasoning that it would have been obvious to turn the strainer upside down. Turning it over would trap the gasoline and send the water out of the outlet. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) Where a proposed modification would render the prior art invention unsatisfactory for its intended purpose, there may be no suggestion or motivation to make the modification.
  2. (B) The rejection is proper, because the applicant must prove that the modification is impossible to perform.
  3. (C) The rejection is proper, because any physically possible rearrangement of a reference is obvious.
  4. (D) Whether the modified reference still works is irrelevant to obviousness.
  5. (E) A change to the principle of operation of the reference always strengthens a prima facie case of obviousness.
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). These are the facts of In re Gordon, where the court reversed because the inverted strainer would be inoperable for its intended purpose. (B), (C) and (D) ignore that rule. (E) is wrong because a modification that changes the principle of operation of a reference makes its teachings insufficient to render the claims prima facie obvious.

MPEP 2143.01, "If a proposed modification would render the prior art invention being modified unsatisfactory for its intended purpose, there may be no suggestion or motivation to make the proposed modification."

Question 6, Proper multiple dependent claim form

An application contains independent claim 1, single dependent claims 2 and 3, and claim 4, which reads, A gadget as in claim 2 or 3, further comprising a latch. Which of the following proposed claim 5 is in proper multiple dependent form?

  1. (A) Claim 5. A gadget according to claims 3 and 4, further comprising a hinge.
  2. (B) Claim 5. A gadget as in claim 2 or 3, further comprising a hinge.
  3. (C) Claim 5. A gadget as in claims 1, 2, or 3, made by a process of claims 3 or 4.
  4. (D) Claim 5. A gadget as in claim 3 or 4, further comprising a hinge.
  5. (E) Claim 5. A gadget according to claim 2 and any claim that follows, further comprising a hinge.
Answer and explanation

Answer (B). Under 35 U.S.C. 112(e), 37 CFR 1.75(c) and MPEP 608.01(n), a multiple dependent claim must refer back in the alternative only to more than one preceding claim, and may not serve as a basis for another multiple dependent claim. B meets both conditions. A is cumulative claiming, which is not permitted. C refers in the alternative to two sets of claims, which is improper. D depends from claim 4, itself a multiple dependent claim. E refers to claims that follow rather than to claims previously set forth, and is not in the alternative.

MPEP 608.01(n), "Generally, a multiple dependent claim is a dependent claim which refers back in the alternative to more than one preceding independent or dependent claim."

Question 7, Mental processes performed on a computer

Inventor Okafor claims a computer system comprising a generic processor programmed to review a list of four credit card transactions and decide whether they came from the same IP address. The specification explains that a fraud analyst traditionally performs the same review by writing the transactions on paper. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The claim cannot recite a mental process, because it requires a processor.
  2. (B) The claim cannot recite a mental process, because the analyst uses pen and paper, which is a physical aid.
  3. (C) Any claim reciting a computer is automatically eligible at Pathway A.
  4. (D) The claim recites a mental process only if the specification expressly uses the words "mental process".
  5. (E) The claim can recite a mental process even though it is claimed as being performed on a computer.
Answer and explanation

Answer (E). Claims can recite a mental process even when performed on a computer, as in Benson and Mortgage Grader, particularly where a concept performed in the mind is merely claimed on a generic computer. A is wrong for that reason. B is wrong because a physical aid such as pen and paper does not negate the mental nature of a step. D is wrong because the examiner reviews the claim and specification under the broadest reasonable interpretation, not for magic words. C is wrong because Pathway A is reserved for claims whose eligibility is self-evident.

MPEP 2106.04(a)(2), "Claims can recite a mental process even if they are claimed as being performed on a computer. The Supreme Court recognized this in Benson, determining that a mathematical algorithm for converting binary coded decimal to pure binary within a computer’s shift register was an abstract idea."

Question 8, Computer readable medium and transitory signals

Applicant Rivera claims "A computer readable medium storing instructions that, when executed, cause a processor to compress image data." The specification states that the medium may be a compact disc, a flash drive or a carrier wave. Under the broadest reasonable interpretation, which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The claim covers a non-statutory embodiment and should be rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 as directed to non-statutory subject matter.
  2. (B) The claim satisfies Step 1 because a compact disc is a manufacture, and a claim needs to cover only one statutory embodiment.
  3. (C) The claim satisfies Step 1 because a carrier wave is physical and real and therefore qualifies as a manufacture.
  4. (D) The examiner should skip Step 1 and proceed directly to Step 2A, because computer readable media are always products.
  5. (E) The claim should be rejected only under 35 U.S.C. 112(b), because the specification lists alternative media.
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). A claim whose broadest reasonable interpretation covers both statutory and non-statutory embodiments fails Step 1, and the MPEP gives the exact example of a medium that can be a compact disc or a carrier wave. B is wrong because covering one statutory embodiment does not cure the non-statutory one. C is wrong because a transitory signal, though physical and real, is not a manufacture or any other category. D is wrong because Step 1 is not skipped for such claims. E is wrong because the defect is one of eligibility under 35 U.S.C. 101, not indefiniteness.

MPEP 2106.03, "Thus, a claim to a computer readable medium that can be a compact disc or a carrier wave covers a non-statutory embodiment and therefore should be rejected under 35 U.S.C. 101 as being directed to non-statutory subject matter."

Question 9, Rebuttal evidence and nexus

In reply to an obviousness rejection, applicant Chen argues in her remarks that her product has sold very well and files a declaration under 37 CFR 1.132 giving sales figures. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) Chen's remarks alone can take the place of factually supported objective evidence.
  2. (B) The examiner may decline to consider the declaration, because secondary considerations are never dispositive.
  3. (C) For the sales evidence to be accorded substantial weight, there must be a nexus, a legally and factually sufficient connection between the evidence and the claimed invention.
  4. (D) Rebuttal evidence may be presented only in the specification as filed.
  5. (E) A presumption of nexus arises whenever a product sells well, whether or not the product embodies and is coextensive with the claimed invention.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). Evidence of secondary considerations receives substantial weight only with a nexus to the claimed invention (Fox Factory). (A) is wrong because arguments cannot take the place of objective evidence. (B) is wrong because it is error not to consider secondary considerations when present (In re Kao). (D) is wrong because rebuttal may be presented in a declaration or otherwise during prosecution. (E) is wrong because a presumption of nexus requires that the product embody the invention and be coextensive with it.

MPEP 2145, "in order for evidence of secondary considerations to be accorded substantial weight, there must be a nexus, i.e., a legally and factually sufficient connection or correspondence between the submitted evidence and the claimed invention."

Question 10, Markedly different characteristics

Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP concerning the markedly different characteristics analysis?

  1. (A) In Roslin, the claimed sheep was a product of nature because it did not possess markedly different characteristics from any farm animals found in nature.
  2. (B) In Roslin, a cloned sheep was held eligible because it was created by human intervention.
  3. (C) The analysis is performed at Step 2B to determine whether a product of nature amounts to significantly more.
  4. (D) In Chakrabarty, the claimed bacterium was a product of nature because bacteria occur in nature.
  5. (E) Once a nature-based product shows a markedly different characteristic, a claim to that product must still be analyzed at Step 2B.
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). Roslin compared the claimed sheep to naturally occurring sheep and found no markedly different characteristics, so the sheep was a product of nature. C is wrong because the analysis is part of Step 2A Prong One. B is wrong because human creation by cloning did not make the sheep markedly different. D is wrong because Chakrabarty found the engineered bacterium had markedly different characteristics and was not a product of nature. E is wrong because for a claim to a single nature-based product, a markedly different characteristic ends the analysis and Step 2B is not necessary.

MPEP 2106.04(c), "Similarly, Roslin relied on a comparison of the claimed sheep to naturally occurring sheep when determining that the claimed sheep was a product of nature because it “does not possess ‘markedly different characteristics from any [farm animals] found in nature.’”"
