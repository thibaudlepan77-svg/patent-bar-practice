Patent bar practice questions on AIA 102 and 103

Five questions in the exam format on AIA novelty, the grace period exceptions, common ownership and obviousness. Each answer quotes the MPEP section or 37 CFR rule it rests on, word for word, checked by script against MPEP Ninth Edition, Revision 01.2024.

Question 1, Effective filing date of claims in a CIP

Inventor Moreau files application P on March 1, 2023 disclosing widget W. On March 1, 2024 she files application C, a proper continuation-in-part of P, adding claim 1 to widget W and claim 2 to an improved widget W2 not supported by P. Both applications are subject to the AIA first inventor to file provisions. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) Claim 1 has an effective filing date of March 1, 2023 if fully supported under 35 U.S.C. 112 by P, and claim 2 has an effective filing date of March 1, 2024.
  2. (B) Both claims have an effective filing date of March 1, 2023, because C claims the benefit of P.
  3. (C) Both claims have an effective filing date of March 1, 2024, because a CIP always takes its own filing date.
  4. (D) The effective filing date of each claim is the date on which the inventor conceived the invention.
  5. (E) The effective filing date of each claim is the date C is published.
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). In a CIP, claims fully supported by the parent take the parent's date, while claims not supported by the parent take the actual filing date of the new application. B and C are wrong because the effective filing date is determined claim by claim. D is wrong because AIA 35 U.S.C. 102 makes no mention of the date of invention. E is wrong because publication date has no role in defining the effective filing date under 35 U.S.C. 100(i)(1).

MPEP 2152.01, "(B) If the application is a continuation-in-part of an earlier U.S. application or international application, any claims in the new application not supported by the specification and claims of the parent application have an effective filing date equal to the actual filing date of the new application."

Question 2, Pre-AIA 102(b) grace period ending on a weekend or holiday

An application subject to pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102 is filed in the USPTO on Tuesday, January 17, 2012. The only reference is the inventor's own journal article, published on Friday, January 14, 2011. January 14, 2012 was a Saturday and Monday, January 16, 2012 was a federal holiday. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The article is a bar under pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b), because it was published more than 365 days before the filing date.
  2. (B) The article is a bar, because 35 U.S.C. 21(b) does not apply to the pre-AIA 1-year grace period.
  3. (C) The article is a bar, because the grace period is extended only to the following Monday whether or not that day is a federal holiday.
  4. (D) The article is not a bar, because a publication by the inventor can never bar the inventor's own application.
  5. (E) The article is not a bar under pre-AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(b), because the 1-year grace period ended on a Saturday and the application was filed on the next succeeding business day.
Answer and explanation

Answer (E). Under Ex parte Olah and 35 U.S.C. 21(b), a publication will not bar a patent if the 1-year grace period ends on a Saturday, Sunday or federal holiday and the U.S. filing date is the next succeeding business day. Here the next business day after Saturday, January 14, 2012 and the Monday holiday was Tuesday, January 17. A and B are wrong for that reason. C is wrong because the extension runs to the next business day, which skips the holiday. D is wrong because one's own disclosure more than 1 year before filing does bar a patent, as In re Katz holds.

MPEP 2133, "However, publications, patents, public uses and sales will not bar a patent if the 1-year grace period otherwise ends on a Saturday, Sunday, or federal holiday and the application’s U.S. filing date is the next succeeding business day."

Question 3, Restoring the right of foreign priority for a design

Designer Tomas Reyes filed a design application in Spain on March 1, 2024. Because of an unintentional docketing error, he filed a U.S. design application on October 1, 2024, seven months later, claiming priority to the Spanish application. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The priority claim is timely because design applications have twelve months to claim foreign priority.
  2. (B) The right of priority may be restored only if the delay was unavoidable.
  3. (C) The right of priority can never be restored for a design application.
  4. (D) The right of priority may be restored upon a petition under 37 CFR 1.55(c) because the U.S. application was filed within two months after the six month period and the delay was unintentional.
  5. (E) The right of priority may be restored for any delay up to fourteen months in a design application.
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). Under MPEP 213.03, a design application must be filed within six months of the foreign application, so A is wrong. Since December 18, 2013 the right of priority may be restored by petition under 37 CFR 1.55(c) if the later application is filed within two months after the period expired and the delay was unintentional, allowing a design to claim priority to a foreign application filed up to eight months earlier. D is therefore correct and C is wrong. B states the wrong standard. E applies the fourteen month figure that governs non-design applications.

MPEP 213.03, "Thus, an application may now validly claim priority under 35 U.S.C. 119(a) through (d) and (f), 172, 365(a) or (b), or 386(a) or (b) to a foreign application filed up to fourteen months earlier (or eight months earlier in the case of a design application)."

Question 4, Anticipation of ranges

Inventor Haddad claims a process run at 330 to 450 degrees C, and the specification shows that this range is critical and that the process operates differently outside it. A reference discloses the same process at 100 to 500 degrees C, with a preferred range of 150 to 350 degrees C, and gives no specific example inside 330 to 450 degrees C. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The claim is anticipated, because any prior art range that overlaps a claimed range always anticipates it.
  2. (B) The claim is anticipated, because the disclosure of a range is a disclosure of its end points and every point within it.
  3. (C) The claim is not anticipated, because a reference range can never anticipate a narrower claimed range.
  4. (D) The claim is not anticipated on these facts, because the reference does not describe the claimed range with sufficient specificity.
  5. (E) The claim is anticipated, because the preferred range of 150 to 350 degrees C touches the claimed range.
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). These facts track Atofina, where a broad reference range with a slightly overlapping preferred range did not describe the critical claimed range with sufficient specificity. A and E are wrong because overlap calls for a case by case determination, not automatic anticipation. B is wrong because the disclosure of a range is no more a disclosure of the end points than of each intermediate point. C is wrong because a broader range can anticipate in some cases, as in ClearValue, where there was no criticality.

MPEP 2131.03, "“[T]he disclosure of a range is no more a disclosure of the end points of the range than it is each of the intermediate points.”"

Question 5, Grace period disclosure and named authors

Two applications subject to the AIA first inventor to file provisions each face a journal article published eight months before their effective filing dates. Application 1 names joint inventors Ames, Brook and Cole, and the article names Ames and Brook as authors. Application 2 names joint inventors Ames and Brook, and the article names Ames, Brook and Cole as authors. The record contains no declaration or other evidence. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The article is prior art under AIA 35 U.S.C. 102(a)(1) against both applications.
  2. (B) The article is not prior art against either application, because every author is named somewhere as an inventor.
  3. (C) It is apparent that the article is a grace period inventor disclosure as to application 1, so it is not prior art there, but it is treated as prior art against application 2 absent evidence that a 102(b)(1) exception applies.
  4. (D) The article is prior art against application 1 but not against application 2.
  5. (E) The article can never be excepted, because a printed publication is not a disclosure by the inventor.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). A grace period publication is not prior art when it names the inventor or a joint inventor and names no additional authors. Application 1 names more inventors than the article has authors, so the exception is apparent. Application 2 names fewer inventors than the article has authors, so the article is treated as prior art unless evidence, such as a 37 CFR 1.130 declaration, shows the exception applies. A, B and D misapply this rule. E is wrong because a publication by the inventor is a classic inventor-originated disclosure.

MPEP 2153.01(a), "If, however, the application names fewer joint inventors than a publication (e.g., the application names as joint inventors A and B, and the publication names as authors A, B and C), it would not be readily apparent from the publication that it is an inventor-originated disclosure"
