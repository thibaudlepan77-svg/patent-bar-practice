Patent bar practice questions on the PCT and national stage

Five questions in the exam format on receiving Office, Chapter II demand, the 30 month limit and national stage filing dates. Each answer quotes the MPEP section or 37 CFR rule it rests on, word for word, checked by script against MPEP Ninth Edition, Revision 01.2024.

Question 1, Time limit for filing a Chapter II demand

An international application claims priority to an application filed March 1, 2024. The international search report and the written opinion of the ISA are transmitted to the applicant on December 16, 2025. Absent any other circumstance, a demand for international preliminary examination must be filed before the expiration of which period in order not to be considered as if it had not been submitted?

  1. (A) The period ending October 1, 2025, 19 months from the priority date.
  2. (B) The period ending March 16, 2026, three months from the transmittal of the search report and written opinion.
  3. (C) The period ending January 1, 2026, 22 months from the priority date.
  4. (D) The period ending September 1, 2026, 30 months from the priority date.
  5. (E) The period ending September 1, 2025, 18 months from the priority date.
Answer and explanation

Answer (B). PCT Rule 54bis.1 sets the later of three months from transmittal of the search report and written opinion, here March 16, 2026, or 22 months from the priority date, here January 1, 2026. The later date controls. A is the date that may matter to keep a 30 month national phase time limit in a few States, not the limit for the demand itself. C is the earlier of the two periods. D is the national stage deadline. E has no basis.

MPEP 1865, "(A) three months from the date of transmittal to the applicant of the international search report and the written opinion; or (B) 22 months from the priority date."

Question 2, Filing date of a U.S. national stage application

Claire files a French application on February 10, 2022. On February 10, 2023 she files an international application designating the United States, claiming priority to the French application. All requirements of 35 U.S.C. 371(c) are met on July 20, 2024. What is the filing date of the resulting U.S. national stage application?

  1. (A) February 10, 2022
  2. (B) August 10, 2024
  3. (C) July 20, 2024
  4. (D) February 10, 2023
  5. (E) The mailing date of the notice of acceptance under 35 U.S.C. 371
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). Under 35 U.S.C. 363 and PCT Article 11(3), the international filing date is also the filing date of the national stage application. A is the priority date, which is not a filing date in the United States. B is the 30 month deadline. C is the 371(c) date, which governs when the application is taken up for action and appears on the filing receipt, but is not the filing date. E has no basis.

MPEP 1893.03(b), "It should be borne in mind that the filing date of the international stage application is also the filing date for the national stage application."

Question 3, 30 month time limit under 37 CFR 1.495

Which of the following is NOT in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP concerning entry into the U.S. national stage under 35 U.S.C. 371?

  1. (A) To avoid abandonment, the basic national fee must be furnished not later than the expiration of thirty months from the priority date.
  2. (B) A copy of the international application need not be furnished if it was previously communicated by the International Bureau.
  3. (C) The thirty month time period for furnishing the basic national fee may be extended under 37 CFR 1.136(a) by paying an extension fee.
  4. (D) If the applicant timely furnishes the items required by 37 CFR 1.495(b), the Office will notify the applicant of an omitted search fee or examination fee and set a time period to provide it.
  5. (E) Under the conditions of 37 CFR 1.495(c)(3), filing of the inventor's oath or declaration may be postponed until the application is otherwise in condition for allowance.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). 37 CFR 1.495(a) states that the thirty month time period may not be extended, so C is the incorrect statement. A and B restate 37 CFR 1.495(b). D restates 37 CFR 1.495(c)(1) and (c)(2). E restates 37 CFR 1.495(c)(3), which allows postponement where an application data sheet identifies each inventor.

MPEP 1893.01(a)(1), "The thirty-month time period set forth in paragraphs (b), (c), (d), (e) and (h) of this section may not be extended."

Question 4, USPTO as receiving Office, competence

Kenji, a national and resident of Japan, and Ana, a national and resident of Brazil, file an international application in the USPTO as receiving Office. Neither applicant is a resident or national of the United States, and both Japan and Brazil are PCT Contracting States. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The USPTO receives the application on behalf of the International Bureau under PCT Rule 19.4 and, upon payment of a fee equal to the transmittal fee, transmits it to the International Bureau, so the date of filing in the USPTO is not lost.
  2. (B) The application is denied an international filing date because the USPTO is not a competent receiving Office for it.
  3. (C) The USPTO is a competent receiving Office because the application was filed in English.
  4. (D) The USPTO returns the papers to the applicants, who must refile in the Japan Patent Office to obtain any international filing date.
  5. (E) The USPTO keeps the application as receiving Office but treats it as a U.S. national application under 35 U.S.C. 111(a).
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). The USPTO is not competent under PCT Rule 19.1(a) when no applicant is a U.S. resident or national, but because at least one applicant is a resident or national of a PCT Contracting State, PCT Rule 19.4 applies and the filing date is preserved. B describes the result only when all applicants are residents and nationals of non-PCT Contracting States. C is wrong, the language does not create competence. D and E describe procedures the MPEP does not provide.

MPEP 1810, "Nonetheless, the date the international application was filed in the USPTO will not be lost as a filing date for the international application if at least one applicant is a resident or national of any PCT Contracting State."
