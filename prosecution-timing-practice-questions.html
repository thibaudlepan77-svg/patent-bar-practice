Patent bar practice questions on prosecution deadlines

Five questions in the exam format on extensions of time, final rejection, RCE, revival and appeal timing. Each answer quotes the MPEP section or 37 CFR rule it rests on, word for word, checked by script against MPEP Ninth Edition, Revision 01.2024.

Question 1, RCE with an IDS as the only submission

A notice of allowance has been mailed in Inventor Okafor's utility application filed in 2021. Before paying the issue fee, Okafor learns of a new reference and files a request for continued examination with the RCE fee and, as the only submission, an information disclosure statement citing that reference. Which of the following is correct?

  1. (A) The submission is improper because an RCE must always include an amendment to the claims
  2. (B) The submission is proper, and the same IDS-only submission would also be proper if the last Office action had been a final rejection
  3. (C) The Office will treat the request as a new application filed under 37 CFR 1.53(b)
  4. (D) The submission is proper because the last Office action is a notice of allowance
  5. (E) The request is improper because prosecution is not closed after a notice of allowance
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). An IDS alone is a sufficient submission under 37 CFR 1.114 in an application subject to a notice of allowance, because no reply under 35 U.S.C. 132 is outstanding. A is wrong because a submission includes an IDS, new arguments or new evidence, not only amendments. B is wrong because after a final rejection a reply is outstanding, so the submission must meet 37 CFR 1.111 and an IDS alone does not. C is wrong because the Office will not convert an RCE into a new application. E is wrong because a notice of allowance closes prosecution under 37 CFR 1.114(b).

MPEP 706.07(h), "Thus, an applicant may file a submission under 37 CFR 1.114 containing only an information disclosure statement (37 CFR 1.97 and 1.98) in an application subject to a notice of allowance under 35 U.S.C. 151, but not in an application where the last Office action is a final rejection"

Question 2, Final rejection based on an IDS filed with the fee

In a first Office action the examiner rejects claims 1 to 10 over Reference X. Applicant Chen replies with arguments only and does not amend any claim. With the reply, and within the period set forth in 37 CFR 1.97(c), Chen files an information disclosure statement with the fee under 37 CFR 1.17(p) citing Reference Y. In the second action the examiner withdraws the rejection over X and rejects claims 1 to 10 over Reference Y. No other new ground of rejection is introduced. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) The second action cannot be made final because Chen did not amend the claims
  2. (B) The second action may be made final only with the written approval of the supervisory patent examiner
  3. (C) The second action may properly be made final, even though the claims were not amended
  4. (D) The second action may be made final only if Chen consents in writing
  5. (E) A second action relying on any art not cited in the first action can never be made final
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). Where information is submitted in an IDS during the 37 CFR 1.97(c) period with the fee, the examiner may rely on it and make the next action final whether or not the claims were amended, provided no other new ground not necessitated by amendment is introduced. A ignores that rule. No supervisory approval or applicant consent is required by MPEP 706.07(a), so B and D are wrong. E is wrong because art submitted in such an IDS is the stated exception to the bar on final actions based on newly cited art.

MPEP 706.07(a), "Where information is submitted in an information disclosure statement during the period set forth in 37 CFR 1.97(c) with a fee, the examiner may use the information submitted, e.g., a printed publication or evidence of public use, and make the next Office action final whether or not the claims have been amended"

Question 3, Extending the time for a reply brief

An examiner's answer without any new ground of rejection is entered in Appellant Kim's application. Kim needs more than two months to prepare a reply brief. Which of the following is in accordance with the patent laws, rules and procedures as related in the MPEP?

  1. (A) Kim may obtain up to five additional months by filing a petition and fee under 37 CFR 1.136(a)
  2. (B) No extension of any kind is available for a reply brief
  3. (C) Kim may seek an extension only under 37 CFR 1.136(b), which requires sufficient cause
  4. (D) Kim may file two reply briefs, the second within two months of the first
  5. (E) An authorization to charge all required fees will act as a constructive petition extending the reply brief period
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). 37 CFR 41.41(c) states that extensions under 37 CFR 1.136(a) do not apply to the reply brief period, and MPEP 1208 states that extensions may be granted under 37 CFR 1.136(b) in applications. A is wrong because 1.136(a) is excluded, and E fails for the same reason since a constructive petition operates only under 1.136(a). B is wrong because 1.136(b) remains available. D is wrong because only a single reply brief may be filed.

MPEP 1208, "Extensions of time to file the reply brief may be granted pursuant to 37 CFR 1.136(b) (for patent applications)"

Question 4, Twice rejected requirement across a continuation

Claim 5 was rejected in Inventor Moreau's parent application. Moreau filed a continuation presenting the same claim 5, and the first Office action in the continuation, a non-final action, again rejects claim 5. Which of the following is correct?

  1. (A) Moreau may file a notice of appeal in the continuation now, even though claim 5 was rejected only once in the continuation
  2. (B) Moreau must wait for a final rejection in the continuation before filing a notice of appeal
  3. (C) Moreau may appeal only in the parent application
  4. (D) Moreau must file a request for continued examination before an appeal can be taken
  5. (E) The notice of appeal must be signed by a registered practitioner to be effective
Answer and explanation

Answer (A). A notice of appeal may be filed once any claim has been twice rejected, whether or not finally, and the two rejections need not occur in the same application. A rejection in a parent followed by a rejection in a continuation suffices, so B, C and D are wrong. Under 37 CFR 41.31(b) the signature requirements of 37 CFR 1.33 and 11.18(a) do not apply to a notice of appeal, so E is wrong.

MPEP 1204, "For example, if any claim was rejected in a parent application, and the claim is again rejected in a continuing application, then applicant can choose to file an appeal in the continuing application, even if the claim was rejected only once in the continuing application."

Question 5, Reply period after final when the advisory action is late

On March 4, 2024, the examiner mails a final rejection setting a 3-month shortened statutory period with the standard advisory action language. Applicant Patel files a first reply on April 22, 2024. The examiner mails an advisory action on June 17, 2024, stating that the reply does not place the application in condition for allowance. Which of the following is correct?

  1. (A) The shortened statutory period expired on June 4, 2024, and any extension fee is calculated from that date
  2. (B) The examiner must enter the April 22 reply as a matter of right because it was filed within two months
  3. (C) Because the advisory action was late, Patel has a new 3-month period running from June 17, 2024
  4. (D) Patel may reply as late as November 17, 2024 by paying a 5-month extension fee
  5. (E) The shortened statutory period expired on June 17, 2024, and any extension fee may be calculated from that date, but the period cannot run later than September 4, 2024
Answer and explanation

Answer (E). When a first reply is filed within two months of a final rejection and the advisory action is mailed after the 3-month period ends, the shortened period expires on the mailing date of the advisory action and extension fees are calculated from that date, but never beyond six months from the final rejection. A describes the case where the advisory action is mailed within three months. B is wrong because an applicant cannot, as a matter of right, amend finally rejected claims after final rejection. C and D ignore the six-month statutory ceiling.

MPEP 714.13, "If the examiner, however, does not mail an advisory action until after the end of 3 months, the shortened statutory period will expire on the date the examiner mails the advisory action and any extension fee may be calculated from that date."
