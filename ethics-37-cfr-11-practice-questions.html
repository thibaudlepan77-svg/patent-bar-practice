Patent bar practice questions on 37 CFR Part 11 ethics

Five questions in the exam format on fees, confidentiality, conflicts, trust accounts, solicitation and designations. Each answer quotes the MPEP section or 37 CFR rule it rests on, word for word, checked by script against MPEP Ninth Edition, Revision 01.2024.

Question 1, Live person-to-person solicitation

Patent attorney Grace, seeking new paying clients, wants to make live telephone calls offering to prepare patent applications. Which call is permitted under 37 CFR 11.703?

  1. (A) A call to an inventor she saw on a television show and has never met.
  2. (B) A call to a family friend in which Grace warns that he will lose his invention unless he signs with her that day.
  3. (C) A call to a former client who has told Grace in writing that she does not wish to be solicited.
  4. (D) A call to the in-house patent manager of a company that routinely files patent applications as part of its business.
  5. (E) A call to a stranger whose application she noticed had been abandoned.
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). 37 CFR 11.703(b)(3) permits live contact with a person who routinely uses for business purposes the type of legal services offered. A and E are contacts with strangers made for pecuniary gain, which 11.703(b) bars. B involves coercion or duress, barred by 11.703(c)(2). C is barred by 11.703(c)(1) even though a prior relationship exists.

37 CFR 11.703, "(3) Person who routinely uses for business purposes the type of legal services offered by the practitioner."

Question 2, Confidentiality and inequitable conduct

During prosecution, patent attorney Nina learns that her client intends to withhold from the examiner a reference the client knows is material, with intent to deceive the Office. Which statement is in accordance with 37 CFR 11.106?

  1. (A) Nina may never reveal information relating to the representation without the client's informed consent.
  2. (B) Nina may reveal information relating to the representation only to prevent reasonably certain death or substantial bodily harm.
  3. (C) Nina may reveal the information only after the patent issues, to rectify the injury.
  4. (D) Nina may reveal information to the extent she reasonably believes necessary to prevent the client from engaging in inequitable conduct before the Office.
  5. (E) Nina may publicize the information to the press to warn competitors.
Answer and explanation

Answer (D). 37 CFR 11.106(b)(2) permits revealing information to the extent reasonably necessary to prevent the client from engaging in inequitable conduct before the Office, and 11.106(c) requires disclosure needed to comply with the duty of disclosure. A ignores those exceptions. B states only one exception, 11.106(b)(1). C is wrong, prevention is permitted before the conduct occurs. E goes beyond what is reasonably necessary.

37 CFR 11.106, "(2) To prevent the client from engaging in inequitable conduct before the Office or from committing a crime or fraud that is reasonably certain to result in substantial injury to the financial interests or property of another"

Question 3, Sharing fees with non-practitioners

Which of the following is permitted under 37 CFR 11.504, absent any arrangement regulated by a State attorney licensing authority?

  1. (A) Paying a non-practitioner engineer a percentage of each patent fee for the clients he brings to the firm.
  2. (B) Forming a partnership with a non-practitioner when the partnership's activities include preparing and prosecuting patent applications.
  3. (C) Including non-practitioner employees in a retirement plan based in part on a profit sharing arrangement.
  4. (D) Allowing an investor who pays the client's fees to direct the practitioner's professional judgment in prosecuting the client's application.
  5. (E) Practicing in a professional corporation in which a non-practitioner serves as a corporate officer.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). 37 CFR 11.504(a)(3) expressly allows non-practitioner employees in a compensation or retirement plan even if based on profit sharing. A is fee sharing with a non-practitioner barred by 11.504(a), and payment for recommendations is also barred by 11.702(b). B is barred by 11.504(b). D is barred by 11.504(c). E is barred by 11.504(d)(2).

37 CFR 11.504, "(3) A practitioner or law firm may include non-practitioner employees in a compensation or retirement plan, even though the plan is based in whole or in part on a profit-sharing arrangement;"

Question 4, Concurrent conflict of interest, consent

Patent agent Omar prosecutes applications for two clients whose products compete, and there is a significant risk that his work for one will be materially limited by his responsibilities to the other. He reasonably believes he can represent both competently and diligently, the representation is not prohibited by law, and neither client asserts a claim against the other before a tribunal. What else does 37 CFR 11.107 require for Omar to continue?

  1. (A) Nothing further, the reasonable belief of the practitioner is sufficient.
  2. (B) A written waiver signed by the examiner handling both applications.
  3. (C) Oral consent from the client he represented first.
  4. (D) Approval from the OED Director.
  5. (E) Informed consent from each affected client, confirmed in writing.
Answer and explanation

Answer (E). Under 37 CFR 11.107(b) a practitioner may represent despite a concurrent conflict only if all four conditions are met, the fourth being informed consent of each affected client, confirmed in writing. A omits that condition. C is wrong because each affected client must consent and the consent must be confirmed in writing. B and D have no basis in the rule.

37 CFR 11.107, "(3) The representation does not involve the assertion of a claim by one client against another client represented by the practitioner in the same litigation or other proceeding before a tribunal; and (4) Each affected client gives informed consent, confirmed in writing."

Question 5, Division of fees between firms

Attorney Ray refers an inventor to patent agent Sue, who practices in a different firm. Ray and Sue wish to divide the fee Sue charges. Under the USPTO Rules of Professional Conduct, which arrangement is permitted?

  1. (A) Ray receives half the fee for the referral alone, without performing services or assuming responsibility, and the client agrees in writing.
  2. (B) The fee is divided in proportion to services performed, the client is not told of the division, and the total fee is reasonable.
  3. (C) Ray assumes joint responsibility for the representation, the client agrees to the arrangement including each share, the agreement is confirmed in writing, and the total fee is reasonable.
  4. (D) The fee is divided in proportion to services performed and the client agrees in writing, although the total fee is unreasonable.
  5. (E) Any division is permitted between two registered practitioners, whatever the client's knowledge.
Answer and explanation

Answer (C). 37 CFR 11.105(e) allows a division between practitioners not in the same firm only if it is proportional to services or each assumes joint responsibility, the client agrees including the shares and the agreement is confirmed in writing, and the total fee is reasonable. C meets all three. A fails the first condition. B lacks client agreement. D fails the reasonableness condition. E ignores all three conditions.

37 CFR 11.105, "(1) The division is in proportion to the services performed by each practitioner or each practitioner assumes joint responsibility for the representation;"
